# DrinksHarbour: full-platform UK validation

[Likely] **The integrated business is commercially coherent, but UK demand and distribution remain unvalidated.** Daily operational software could supply trustworthy stock to a marketplace that generates additional tenant business. Seven monetisation opportunities do not establish seven functioning revenue streams.

Assumption: UK remains the assessment market; relocation and incorporation are unconfirmed.

Assumption: paying tenants include beverage retailers, bars, lounges and distributors; consumers buy beverages or venue services. Which UK segment adopts first is unresolved. The earlier bottle-shop-only assumption and full-product pivot recommendation are superseded.

## What is actually being validated?

[Certain] The user-provided architecture defines a central Product catalogue, with each tenant owning SubProducts containing its prices, stock and variants. Paying tenants receive operational software and branded selling channels. Marketplace orders route to fulfilling tenants.

[Likely] The proposed reinforcing cycle is:

**Daily ERM use → accurate available offers → reliable marketplace fulfilment → incremental tenant revenue → stronger reasons to retain the ERM.**

This cycle fails if stock is inaccurate, shoppers do not arrive, fulfilment is unreliable or commissions consume merchants' incremental profit. It must be observed, not assumed from the architecture.

The wider model includes advertising, loyalty, venue services, subscriptions/gifting and later direct distribution. Loyalty initially drives retention and carries a reward cost; it is not independent revenue without a paying mechanism. Advertising needs an audience; distribution needs working capital. Do not require these future streams to make the initial model viable.

## 1. Who pays, and will they pay today?

[Likely] The tenant buyer is the owner or operations manager responsible for purchases, stock, sales, staff and customer service. The consumer buyer wants a particular beverage or occasion fulfilled reliably. These are distinct acquisition decisions.

[Certain] The project's [7 October operating record](../docs/legal/investors/valuation-assumptions.md) records founder-confirmed **two paying Nigerian tenants and no online sales at that time**. These are dated representations, not freshly verified receipts. They are initial evidence for ERM willingness to pay, not proof of UK demand, retention or marketplace conversion.

[Guessing] Keep £49/£99/£199 as UK test prices from the earlier proposal. Seek payment for the combination of operational control and selling capability, with explicit scope. Do not promise marketplace orders to secure the subscription. UK willingness to pay today remains **unproven**, so budget it as zero until paid commitments exist.

## 2. Why has nobody built this?

[Certain] Several businesses already cover important parts:

- [Wine Owners and Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/uk/integrations/wine-owners/) combine specialist wine-business management with POS, reporting and multichannel operations. Integrated beverage operations are not new by themselves.
- [Lightspeed Restaurant](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054950934-Introduction-to-the-Back-Office) documents staff operations, inventory, customer/loyalty functions and integrations for ordering and reservations. Venues already have broad operating alternatives.
- [Faire](https://www.faire.com/en-gb/how-faire-works) combines a wholesale marketplace with transaction tools and commercial incentives. It is an adjacent B2B model, not DrinksHarbour's tenant or consumer architecture.
- [Drizly's own closure notice](https://drizly.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/23238173058701-Drizly-Farewell-FAQs) confirms consumer operations were disabled on 28 March 2024. This proves closure of a related alcohol marketplace, not that all such marketplaces fail or what specifically caused its closure.

[Likely] Test whether DrinksHarbour connects operations, selling and customer demand better at a viable total cost. These examples establish component adoption, not profitability of the complete proposition. Feature count does not prove differentiation.

No unique UK timing advantage is established. Reusable software reduces development effort, not consumer acquisition costs automatically.

## 3. What is the distribution advantage?

[Likely] Merchant onboarding could seed marketplace supply without separately convincing every business to maintain another catalogue. That is a potential supply advantage. It does not seed consumer demand automatically, and existing merchant customers are not all incremental customers.

For the first 100 paying tenants, test founder-led sales, importer/accountant introductions and tenant referrals in one reachable area. Estimate: 1,000 qualified approaches × 20% demonstrations × 50% paid conversion = 100 tenants. Those are hypotheses; record actual conversion, labour, onboarding cost and retention before expanding the funnel.

For consumers, test a mixed celebration order, hard-to-find bottle or venue booking in the same area. Measure incremental contribution and repeat purchases. Avoid promoting all seven streams simultaneously.

## Three validation experiments

Proposed tests only; no outreach, charges or launch authorised here. Each takes under two weeks to its initial decision and has a **US$150-equivalent cash cap**, excluding separately logged founder labour. Thresholds are proposed decision rules, not benchmarks.

1. **Integrated paid demand.** Interview 12 UK owners across retail, hospitality and distribution; demonstrate the ERM plus catalogue/storefront/marketplace flow. Ask what they would replace, retain and pay for. Pass: three unrelated businesses in one segment pay for a clearly scoped, deliverable pilot through an eligible billing arrangement, without guaranteed orders. Ten days; estimated cash ceiling £75, subject to USD cap. Do not charge for gated or unavailable capabilities.
2. **Operational supply quality.** With three consenting businesses, map 100 redacted stock lines each into a sandbox catalogue and tenant offers; simulate a sale, stock adjustment, cancellation and refund. Pass: zero false Product merges or tenant data leaks, at least 99% audited stock/price accuracy, and each operator completes the flow unaided. Seven days; estimated ceiling £50, subject to USD cap. Small-sample success is not production certification.
3. **Incremental customer value.** With approved fulfilment arrangements, test one purchasing occasion across two tenants, disclosing split fulfilment and delivery costs. Use existing merchant checkouts if platform payments are unapproved. Pass: ten attributable completed orders, five new-to-merchant customers, positive actual variable contribution and both tenants willing to continue. Ten days; estimated ceiling £100, subject to USD cap. This fallback validates demand, not the platform's single-cart payment flow; observe returns and repeat purchases later.
