## Confirmed loss limit and proposed spending gate

[Likely] Ring-fence $500 as an emergency reserve and limit planned cumulative net cash loss to $1,500 over six months: **$250/month on average**. Reserve use is not preapproved by this recommendation. The full $2,000 cap permits about $333/month average without a reserve, but spending that much leaves no buffer.

Net operating cash loss = business cash outflows minus earned/usable operating receipts; exclude founder funding and borrowing as revenue. Account for liabilities and future service obligations before treating receipts as usable. Track economic profit separately, including unpaid founder labour and accrued expenses. A profitable receipt can fund additional delivery expense, but does not justify spending tax, tenant settlements or wallet money.

Proposed cumulative planned-loss checkpoints: month1 $250; month2 $500; month3 $750; month4 $1,000; month5 $1,250; month6 $1,500. These are spending gates, not a requirement to lose money each month. Unused allowance carries forward only after a fresh cash review. Do not commit beyond the cap through contracts or credit.

For the first30 days, use existing software, remote demonstrations and direct introductions; keep total net loss within $250. If unavoidable monthly net burn already exceeds $250, the plan does not fit: reduce cancellable costs, collect more earned revenue or secure customer-funded delivery before expansion. Avoid new hiring, inventory purchases, travel or broad advertising from this limited buffer. A UK pilot must fit the remaining allowance or be funded by an anchor customer/partner. Actual costs must establish feasibility.

## Commercial priorities

1. **Get the existing operation's numbers first.** The 7 October valuation assumptions record two paying Nigerian tenants and no marketplace sales at that time. Reconcile actual receipts and current usage. Price-list MRR is not collected MRR. Never mix NGN and GBP without explicit conversion assumptions.
2. **Make recurring operations revenue carry the recurring cost base.** Prioritise tenants who use several existing workflows, renew and need repeatable support. If near-term profit is the priority, deepen the market with actual customers before funding UK expansion, unless a UK anchor customer or partner funds a bounded pilot. Do not assume existing Nigerian customers validate UK prices.
3. **Sell the complete relevant package to one initial buyer segment and region.** The platform can serve retail, hospitality and distribution; the first sales process should be repeatable. Choose the segment by paid uptake, onboarding effort and support cost. This is rollout sequencing, not reducing DrinksHarbour to inventory software.
4. **Charge for implementation.** Estimate: four hours at £25/hour plus £25 import/tool costs = £125 delivery cost; a £250 setup fee leaves £125 before sales overhead and tax. Test this quote with a fixed scope. Bespoke migration, hardware and additional training require separate quotes. Setup cash pays for onboarding; it is not recurring revenue or instant profit before delivery.
5. **Validate prices before discounting.** Earlier £49/99/199 monthly prices and founding discounts are proposals. Start by asking qualified buyers to pay list price for deliverable scope. Discount only against measured objections or lower service scope, because automatic discounts reduce contribution. Honour existing contracts. For mature recurring service, target at least 70% direct gross margin as a management rule, not an industry benchmark.
6. **Keep development commercially accountable.** Maintain security, reliability, money correctness and legal readiness. Prioritise other work when it removes a documented sales blocker, prevents churn or reduces measured cost across several tenants. Separately funded custom work must include future maintenance cost. Avoid an unpriced bespoke product for every customer.

## Sequence and stop rules

- **Days 1 to 14:** reconcile cash and costs; speak to current tenants; select one initial UK segment if proceeding. Obtain three paid, scoped pilots through an eligible arrangement before assuming UK demand. Do not charge for unavailable features.
- **Days 15 to 45:** standardise onboarding, document outcomes and measure renewals. Target six paying UK tenants by day 60, subject to actual founder capacity. A national campaign or new salaried team is premature without a financed plan.
- **Days 46 to 90:** target ten paying UK tenants and test local marketplace demand. At the illustrative £60.55 contribution, ten tenants support only £605.50 of monthly fixed costs. This is validation, not a forecast of profit by day 90.
- Apply the confirmed $2,000 six-month maximum loss and proposed $1,500 planned-loss gate above. Reconcile available cash and obligations before any spending. Preserve tax, customer-liability, contracted-service and orderly-closure funds.
- If 20 qualified demonstrations yield fewer than three paid pilots, revise the buyer, offer or channel before further expansion. If support economics miss margin targets for two consecutive months, fix scope/onboarding or test higher new-customer pricing before hiring. If tenants do not renew, diagnose the cause before buying more leads.
- Add advertising after measurable audience demand, gifting when incremental fulfilment earns a margin, and distribution only after separate stock/working-capital economics. Loyalty should have a funded redemption budget. Retain the wider platform thesis without making all seven streams prerequisites for survival.
