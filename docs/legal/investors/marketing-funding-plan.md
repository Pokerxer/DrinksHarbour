# Advertising funding while preserving ownership

Recommend a **staged non-convertible company loan**, with a maximum facility of NGN 5,000,000 and an initial draw of NGN 1,000,000. The lender receives repayment and negotiated interest, **zero equity, no conversion right and no warrant**. The purpose is to finance measured customer acquisition, not to sell a permanent stake in DrinksHarbour.

This supersedes the recommendation to use the 35m-cap convertible draft for this advertising purpose. The existing Word contract is retained as a historical alternative; it has not been rewritten or executed by this plan. The user's preference against approximately 14% dilution is authoritative. Do not inflate the business valuation merely to disguise dilution. No lender has accepted these terms.

The user subsequently requested deliverable documents. Separate review drafts now exist: `drinksharbour-marketing-loan.md` and its Advertising Loan Agreement Word copy; `marketing-funding-summary.md` and its Advertising Funding Plan Word copy. These implement the zero-equity direction while preserving the earlier convertible alternative. This full working plan supplies context and alternatives, not additional operative terms unless expressly signed into the agreement. Parties, funding dates and affordability remain to be confirmed.

## Recommended commercial terms

| Term | Proposed position |
|---|---|
| Facility ceiling | NGN 5m; debt accrues only on funds actually received |
| Draws | 1m pilot, then 2m and 2m subject to agreed evidence and cash-flow gates |
| Ownership | No shares, conversion, warrants or perpetual revenue/profit participation |
| Interest | Opening proposal of 8% yearly, simple, non-compounding; not a Nigerian market quote |
| Scheduled term | 36 months for each draw, measured from its funding date |
| Grace | No scheduled payments in months 1–6 of each draw; interest accrues separately |
| Repayment | Months 7–36: thirty equal principal instalments, plus one-thirtieth of grace interest, plus interest on that month's opening unpaid principal |
| Interest convention for this proposal | 8%/12 per month; final agreement must state dates, day convention and rounding clearly |
| Early repayment | Allowed without penalty; pay accrued interest and unpaid principal only |
| Undrawn amounts | No interest or commitment fee; lender obligation to fund later stages must be expressly agreed |
| Security and guarantees | Opening proposal: unsecured; no founder guarantee or security over code/domain |
| Reporting | Monthly campaign spend, paid collections, acquisition/retention and repayment forecast |
| Distress | Prompt notice and negotiated written restructuring; no automatic equity conversion or automatic waiver of company debt |

Eight percent is a negotiating offer, not a rate the lender is obliged to accept. An unsecured early-stage company with only two customers offers substantial credit risk; the lender might require more return or refuse. Do not agree a larger payment, security over essential IP or personal guarantee without assessing the consequences. Protecting equity does not eliminate debt obligations or insolvency risk.

## Pilot and later draw gates

The founder confirmed the advertising targets **both paying businesses and online shoppers**. Allocate the first 1m over approximately eight to twelve weeks, with separate attribution and ledgers: 600k for business acquisition and 400k for shopper acquisition. This is a proposed allocation, not actual campaign expenditure. No campaign copy, ad account access or paid launch is included in this proposal.

| Pilot use | Businesses | Shoppers | Total budget |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Targeted paid advertising | 250,000 | 200,000 | 450,000 |
| Creative production and landing-page improvements | 100,000 | 50,000 | 150,000 |
| Demo/onboarding or shopper conversion support | 200,000 | 50,000 | 250,000 |
| Attribution, reporting and campaign operations | 40,000 | 60,000 | 100,000 |
| Contingency | 10,000 | 40,000 | 50,000 |
| Total | **600,000** | **400,000** | **1,000,000** |

Release the second 2m only after all proposed gates are met:

- At least four **additional business-campaign-attributed** tenants are active, have paid twice at agreed recurring prices, and are not just free trials or existing customers relabelled.
- Fully loaded business acquisition cost is at most 150k per retained new tenant: allocated media, agency/creative, sales follow-up and onboarding costs divided by those customers. At a complete 600k business pilot spend and four tenants, CAC is 150k. Shared costs must be allocated once; shopper expenses must not be hidden in the tenant denominator.
- Actual contribution after provider fees, variable support and relevant discounts indicates a CAC payback of at most six months. Recurring revenue must exclude one-time fees and taxes.
- A current downside cash forecast supports existing and proposed debt service alongside operations. Cash forecasts include failed collections and the overlapping draw schedules.
- The lender receives the agreed evidence and funds only under its signed commitment. Passing a KPI is not itself a funding promise from a lender who has not committed.
- Shopper campaigns have documented positive contribution after allocated acquisition spend on a settled cohort, or their remaining budget is paused and reallocated under the signed budget consent process. A successful tenant channel does not establish that shopper ads are profitable. Later funding must specify which proven channel it finances rather than blindly doubling both budgets.

The last 2m requires equivalent gates for the expanded paid cohort, measured retention, and an updated cash forecast. Do not spend to exhaust a budget. Pause campaigns that fail the gates and leave later funds undrawn. Default thresholds are negotiating and experiment-design assumptions, not proven DrinksHarbour conversion performance.

## Acquisition economics

At the earlier assumed mix, base subscription revenue per tenant is 45k. Less approximately 775 provider fees and 5k variable service cost leaves **39,225 monthly contribution before fixed overhead**. At 150k CAC the illustrative payback is **3.82 months**. At 300k CAC it is **7.65 months**, failing the proposed six-month gate. Retention and actual variable costs still need measurement.

Four such additional tenants would add 180k gross recurring subscriptions and approximately 156,900 contribution before fixed overhead. This is a hypothetical target, not expected campaign performance. It would exceed the approximately 41,333 first pilot payment, but any extra staff or overhead reduces the headroom. The campaign can fail, which is why full funding is staged.

For online-shopper acquisition, calculate customer-order proceeds minus tenant payout, payment/payout costs, refunds, discounts, delivery subsidy and variable support. Compare **that contribution** with fully loaded acquisition cost and verified repeat purchases. Do not count full marketplace GMV as available company cash. The earlier illustrative 11% platform share and 8% variable-cost budget included2% acquisition already. When modelling this campaign's actual acquisition budget separately, remove that2% line first: assumed non-acquisition costs become6%, leaving5% before acquisition and fixed overhead. At that margin, 20m fulfilled GMV would generate1m to cover1m of acquisition spend. Use one acquisition-cost line rather than charging both the budget and a second percentage against the same orders.

For an illustrative 25k basket, an 11% share gives 2,750 gross earnings. The assumed non-acquisition costs (2% provider/payout, 3% delivery/service subsidy and 1% reserve) total1,500, leaving1,250 before shopper acquisition. An initial goal of at most625 fully loaded acquisition cost per fulfilled order would leave at least625 contribution. This is a proposed test threshold, not a verified ad price or purchase rate. Repeat orders count only when fulfilled and attributable; do not assume future lifetime value to rescue a losing first-order campaign. At those assumptions, 400k shopper spend requires320 fulfilled orders just to break even on acquisition, or640 at the625 target. ## Alternatives and their limits

**Revenue-linked loan if fixed payments are unacceptable.** Negotiate payments as a share of defined collected company revenue or contribution, ending at a fixed repayment cap. For example, 10% monthly collections retained by the company, with total scheduled payments capped at 1.2 times actual draws, would mean a 6m total target for a fully drawn 5m facility. This is a finance charge, not equity or an annual 20% interest rate. The definition must exclude tenant payouts, taxes, wallet/gift liabilities and refunded amounts; specify whether provider/fulfilment costs reduce the payment base. Payment timing materially changes the effective annual financing cost.

This can move slowly: at a 120k monthly payment base, 10% is 12k and a 6m target takes 500 months without growth or grace. At 2m, 10% is 200k and takes 30 payment months. With a three-month grace and a 36-month long-stop, the average base over the remaining 33 months must be approximately 1.818m to clear 6m. A maturity balloon or extension is a real issue, not solved by calling the loan revenue-linked. Before signing, fix the long-stop, unpaid-balance treatment and default/exit terms; no automatic forgiveness or assumption that the lender accepts indefinite repayment. Given current receipts, this is an alternative to negotiate, not an approved affordable product.

**If the lender insists on equity:** negotiate a smaller staged investment or decline if permanent ownership is unacceptable. A higher valuation cap alone gives no fixed maximum dilution: interest and the discount can change shares. As an illustration, limiting a 5.8m conversion balance to 5% before other dilution would require a 110.2m pre-money pricing basis, since 5.8/(110.2+5.8)=5%. That arithmetic does not establish that DrinksHarbour is worth 110.2m. A hard maximum equity allotment would require an express negotiated term and clear treatment of any remaining debt, not a disguised cap change.
