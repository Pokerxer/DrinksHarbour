# DrinksHarbour Investor Agreement

DRAFT FOR NEGOTIATION AND LEGAL REVIEW

Share Subscription and Shareholders Agreement

This Agreement records a proposed investment in the company operating the DrinksHarbour technology business. The Investor subscribes for newly issued ordinary shares, and the parties agree funding, governance, information and transfer rights. All bracketed fields must be completed and the schedules agreed before execution.

This Agreement covers only the ordinary equity subscription described in its completed schedules. A separate advertising loan, convertible loan or software licence does not form part of this Agreement unless expressly identified in a signed amendment. No valuation or funding figure in another proposal is incorporated by reference.

## 2 Subscription and Completion

2.1 The Investor agrees to subscribe for the Subscription Shares for the investment amount in Schedule 1. The shares rank equally with existing ordinary shares for voting, dividends and distributions, subject to applicable law and the Company's articles. No preference, guaranteed yield, redemption right or anti-dilution price adjustment is created by this Agreement.

2.2 Completion is conditional on: verified incorporation and capacity of the Company; delivery of current articles and an accurate fully diluted capitalisation table; required corporate approvals and compliance with or valid waiver of applicable pre-emption rights; signature or necessary consent of existing holders; satisfactory identity and source-of-funds checks; agreed disclosure letter and budget; documented ownership or adequate licences for material platform IP; and any regulatory approvals required for this transaction. A condition cannot be waived where mandatory law requires it.

2.3 The Company shall provide the conditions documents at least five Business Days before Completion. The Investor may refuse Completion if a condition remains unsatisfied or a material warranty is inaccurate. Any waiver of a contractual condition must be express and written.

2.4 At Completion, the Investor transfers cleared funds to the verified Company account and the Company validly allots the Subscription Shares, enters the Investor in its register of members and delivers signed allotment resolutions and an updated capitalisation table. The parties shall coordinate payment and allotment through [AGREED CLOSING MECHANISM OR SOLICITOR ESCROW]. Subscription funds received early must remain segregated and unused pending Completion.

2.5 The Company shall deliver a share certificate or legally permitted equivalent within ten Business Days after Completion, and make required allotment, beneficial ownership and related filings within applicable statutory periods. It shall provide filing evidence promptly. Counsel shall confirm the current filing periods before execution.

2.6 If Completion has not occurred by the long-stop date, either non-defaulting party may terminate the uncompleted subscription by written notice. The Company shall return any unallotted subscription money within five Business Days, without deduction except a bank charge expressly agreed in writing. This does not extinguish claims for prior breach.

2.7 Partial funding creates no entitlement to the full Subscription Shares. A partial closing or staged funding requires a signed amendment specifying the shares issued for each payment, objective milestone tests, evidence, deadlines, dispute procedure and consequences of non-payment. No shares are issued for an unpaid future commitment.

## 3 Nature of the investment

3.1 The investment is equity in the Company operating DrinksHarbour. It gives no direct ownership of a tenant's business, stock, customer account or beverage catalogue entry. The Investor receives only the shares and rights expressly agreed here.

3.2 Equity is illiquid and may lose its entire value. No party guarantees profit, dividends, capital repayment, a funding round, an acquisition or a listing. Projections are estimates; this acknowledgement does not excuse fraud or a false contractual warranty.

## 4 Business scope and use of funds

4.1 DrinksHarbour is a beverage technology business intended to provide a central marketplace, tenant business management software, storefronts and operational tools. Its contemplated revenue streams include marketplace commissions, software subscriptions, advertising, venue services, gifting and reorder subscriptions, and later distribution. This description does not represent that every feature is launched or profitable.

The platform's paying beverage businesses are Tenants. The platform maintains a central product catalogue; Tenants operate their own selling listings, pricing and stock. This technology investment conveys no proprietary interest in a Tenant's separate assets or in external suppliers.

4.2 The Company shall use the investment for the acquisition campaign budget in Schedule 3, including paid advertising, campaign creative and landing improvements, tenant onboarding, shopper conversion support, attribution and contingency. Customer money, tenant settlement balances and taxes collected for remittance must be accounted for separately from Company revenue and investment capital.

4.3 Investment funds must not be used for personal expenses, undisclosed founder debt repayment or distributions to shareholders outside this Agreement and applicable law. Founder compensation and any related-party payments must be disclosed and approved in the budget or under clause 6.

4.4 Management may reallocate spending within the approved budget, subject to the threshold in Schedule 1. Reallocations above that threshold require board approval and, while applicable, Investor consent. A departure from a forecast or target alone is not a breach; diversion of funds or a knowingly false progress report may be.

## 5 Intellectual property and operating safeguards

5.1 The Company shall obtain written assignments of all founder, employee and contractor rights in the source code, product designs, brand assets and other proprietary work necessary to operate DrinksHarbour, to the extent those rights are assignable. Any third-party, open-source or pre-existing rights excluded from assignment must be identified in the disclosure letter with their licence terms and operational limitations.

5.2 The Company shall control the business domain, hosting, repositories, payment accounts and material service accounts through authorised Company administrators. It shall maintain backups, access controls and a documented handover process. Founders must not retain an undisclosed exclusive ability to disable the business.

5.3 The Investor acquires no personal right to copy, sell or use platform code, confidential information or customer data. Share ownership does not entitle the Investor to production credentials or unrestricted access to tenant records.

5.4 The Company shall maintain reasonable cybersecurity measures, tenant separation and lawful handling of personal information. Investor reporting shall use aggregate or de-identified information where practical. Material security incidents, loss of essential IP rights or loss of an essential operating licence shall be reported under clause 7.

5.5 The Company shall comply with applicable consumer, data protection, payment, advertising and beverage-related rules relevant to its actual activities, including lawful age restrictions on alcohol sales. No provision assumes that an unfinished software gate constitutes legal compliance.

## 6 Management and shareholder protections

6.1 The board manages the Company and appoints management in accordance with law and the articles. The Investor has no authority to bind the Company or direct daily operations solely by holding shares. The agreed board composition at Completion is [NAMES AND APPOINTMENT RIGHTS]. No Investor board seat is granted unless expressly entered here and implemented in the articles.

6.2 If Schedule 1 grants an observer right, the Investor may nominate one non-voting observer to attend board meetings and receive the same meeting materials. The board may exclude that observer only to protect legal privilege, address an actual conflict of interest or comply with law, giving reasons where lawful. An observer has no director vote or power.

6.3 While the Investor meets the consent holding threshold, the Company and signing Shareholders shall not approve or implement the following without the Investor's prior written consent: an issue of shares, options or convertible securities other than grants within an expressly approved option pool; a change adversely affecting the Investor's share rights; amendment of the articles affecting these rights; sale or exclusive disposal of material platform IP or substantially all business assets; a merger, winding up or fundamental change in business; borrowing outside the approved budget above the debt threshold; a related-party transaction above the stated threshold; or a dividend, buyback or capital reduction.

6.4 Consent is an additional contractual requirement and does not replace corporate approvals. Silence is not consent. The Investor shall respond with approval or reasons within ten Business Days after receiving sufficient information. A refusal may be discussed under clause 14; lawful urgent action necessary to prevent insolvency or comply with a legal duty is not prohibited by this clause, with prompt notice to the Investor.

6.5 Before a cash issue of new shares, the Company shall offer the Investor its proportionate entitlement on the same terms, with at least fifteen Business Days to accept, subject to applicable law and any stricter articles requirement. Any permitted exception, including an agreed employee option pool, must be expressly identified in Schedule 2. If the Investor does not participate, its percentage may dilute.

6.6 Each signing Shareholder shall exercise its lawful voting rights to implement this Agreement. The parties shall adopt consistent articles at Completion. Mandatory law prevails; between signing parties, a contractual inconsistency must be resolved by lawful amendment, not by treating an invalid corporate act as valid.

## 7 Information rights

7.1 Within thirty days after each quarter, the Company shall provide management accounts, cash balance and runway, budget variances, and a concise progress report. Reports should distinguish gross marketplace transaction value from Company revenue and include available tenant subscription, retention and settlement indicators.

7.2 Annual financial statements shall be supplied within 120 days after the financial year end, with an audit where legally required or otherwise agreed. Reasonable inspection of financial records is permitted on ten Business Days' notice, during business hours, subject to confidentiality, privilege and data protection.

7.3 The Company shall notify the Investor promptly, ordinarily within five Business Days after management becomes aware, of material litigation, suspected material fraud, insolvency risk or a material operational or security incident. This contractual period does not extend any shorter legal reporting deadline.

## 8 Dividends and exits

8.1 Dividends may be declared only from legally distributable amounts following required approvals and prudent consideration of working capital. Ordinary shares participate according to their rights and holdings. There is no automatic distribution of marketplace GMV or tenant subscription receipts to the Investor.

8.2 An exit may occur through a permitted share sale, acquisition, lawful Company buyback or other agreed transaction. No exit date or minimum sale value is promised. A buyback is subject to law, available funds and required approvals; the Investor cannot demand that the Company repay the investment merely because an anticipated exit has not occurred.

8.3 On winding up, creditors and legally senior claims are paid before any ordinary shareholder distribution. Remaining amounts are distributed according to the share rights. These ordinary shares have no separate liquidation preference.

## 9 Share transfers

9.1 No party may transfer shares in breach of law, the articles or existing binding restrictions disclosed at Completion. A permitted transferee must first sign a deed of adherence to the continuing shareholder provisions. Statutory restrictions on membership and transfers remain applicable.

9.2 A Shareholder proposing a third-party sale shall give the other Shareholders a written notice identifying the buyer, number of shares, price and material terms. The other Shareholders have twenty Business Days to elect to acquire the offered shares proportionately, subject to mandatory rights and any stricter articles provisions. If not fully accepted, the seller may sell the remaining shares within sixty Business Days on terms no more favourable to the buyer, failing which a new notice is required.

9.3 If a proposed third-party sale would transfer more than 50% of voting shares or otherwise control of the Company, each non-selling Shareholder may require the buyer to purchase the same proportion of its shares on the same economic terms. The selling parties shall give at least twenty Business Days' notice. A control sale must not complete unless the buyer honours properly exercised tag-along rights.

9.4 No drag-along right is granted by this draft. A right requiring minority holders to sell must be separately negotiated, approved by the affected holders and reflected in the articles and definitive agreement. No Shareholder is compelled to sell merely by a board decision.

9.5 Founder vesting, compulsory transfers and good-leaver or bad-leaver rules do not apply unless separately agreed in writing with the affected founders. No automatic forfeiture of fully paid shares occurs on departure or breach.

C means the verified issued ordinary shares immediately before this subscription, after any separately approved reorganisation necessary for exact whole-share allotment. ## Schedule 3 Acquisition budget and milestones

Budget period: the first six months after Completion. The full NGN 5,000,000 subscription is paid at Completion; the spending phases below are internal budget releases and do not defer or condition the Investor's 5% share allotment. This proposed acquisition budget becomes binding when the parties sign the completed Agreement.

| Use of investment | NGN allocation | Intended delivery or period |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paid advertising | 2,250,000 | Months 1 to 6; business and shopper campaigns |
| Creative and landing improvements | 750,000 | Initial assets in month 1; optimisation through month 6 |
| Demos onboarding and conversion support | 1,250,000 | Months 1 to 6; business demos and shopper support |
| Attribution and campaign operations | 500,000 | Tracking by end of month 1; monthly cost and results ledger |
| Campaign contingency | 250,000 | Retained for documented campaign contingencies |
| Total | 5,000,000 | Six months after Completion |

Audience allocation: NGN 3,000,000 for paying beverage business acquisition and NGN 2,000,000 for shoppers. Within the five categories above, business/shopper amounts respectively are: advertising NGN 1,250,000 / 1,000,000; creative NGN 500,000 / 250,000; support NGN 1,000,000 / 250,000; attribution NGN 200,000 / 300,000; contingency NGN 50,000 / 200,000. Shared costs are allocated once. Amounts are gross spending ceilings inclusive of applicable taxes and charges; unspent money remains Company cash.

Phase 1: release up to NGN 1,000,000 for months 1 to 3, comprising NGN 600,000 for businesses and NGN 400,000 for shoppers. Category ceilings are NGN 450,000 advertising, NGN 150,000 creative, NGN 250,000 support, NGN 100,000 attribution and NGN 50,000 contingency. Establish separate campaign attribution and spending records in month 1; use a NGN 30,000 shopper media test within the advertising ceiling before expanding shopper spend.

Phase 2: release up to NGN 2,000,000 for months 4 and 5 after a documented board review of the pilot. Business reporting target: at least four additional campaign-attributed active paying Tenants, each with two paid recurring collections; all-in acquisition cost at most NGN 150,000 per retained Tenant and contribution payback within six months. Shopper review: report fulfilled orders and settled contribution after tenant payouts, payment costs, refunds, delivery and support, discounts and allocated acquisition cost. Expand only channels showing positive settled cohort contribution. Registrations, clicks and gross marketplace sales are not substitutes for these results.

Phase 3: release up to NGN 2,000,000 in month 6 after a further board review of retained paid customers, settled channel contribution, spending to date and remaining cash. Phases 2 and 3 each use up to NGN 1,200,000 for businesses and NGN 800,000 for shoppers; each phase's category ceilings are NGN 900,000 advertising, NGN 300,000 creative, NGN 500,000 support, NGN 200,000 attribution and NGN 100,000 contingency. Pause underperforming channels and retain their unused allocations pending a documented decision.

Provide the Investor a monthly budget-versus-actual report within fifteen days after month end, including paid tenant retention, all-in acquisition costs, fulfilled-order contribution and remaining campaign cash; provide a six-month review within thirty days after the budget period ends. Targets are planning and spending-review criteria, not guaranteed revenue, repayment obligations or grounds to forfeit validly allotted shares.

No founder remuneration, shareholder debt repayment, related-party payment or Investor adviser reimbursement is authorised by this budget. Such payments require separate written approval under the Agreement. General payroll, core platform redevelopment and unrelated operating overhead are outside this campaign budget. Until the Schedule 1 reallocation threshold is completed, any transfer between budget categories or audience allocations, change in purpose, or extension beyond six months requires board approval and written Investor consent. Record the reason and revised totals; never exceed the NGN 5,000,000 ceiling without a separately approved funding source.
