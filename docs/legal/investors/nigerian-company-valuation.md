## Revenue and profit model

Subscriptions provide recurring fees. Marketplace orders can generate commissions or gross markup spreads under the actual tenant agreement. The full customer basket is not Company revenue: tenant settlements must be deducted. Provider charges, fulfilment support, refunds, losses and acquisition costs then reduce contribution; salaries, infrastructure, maintenance, administration and tax reduce profit further.

The platform also contemplates sponsored advertising, venue services, gifting and reorder subscriptions. These opportunities support the commercial narrative but are not treated as current earnings. Loyalty supports retention; direct distribution is a later capital-intensive phase. No forecast customer count or revenue is guaranteed.

There is no verified current profit figure. Actual receipts, expenses, cash, debts, retention and acquisition costs remain unavailable. The proposed value is approximately **41.67 times** the current annualised subscription baseline, so it relies heavily on software utility and future execution rather than established earnings.

## What a NGN 5 million transaction means

Under the accompanying Nigerian Advertising Loan Agreement, a NGN 5 million fully drawn loan gives the lender **0% ownership**. NGN 60 million is recorded for context only. Principal and interest remain payable under the loan's dates and repayment terms, even if campaign results disappoint. There is no conversion on maturity or default.

If the parties separately choose a new cash equity subscription, these examples show the basic dilution before fees, option-pool changes, other share issues or special share rights. They are illustrations, not an equity offer or operative loan terms.

| New cash equity | Post-money value | New investor ownership |
|---|---|---|
| NGN 1,000,000 | NGN 61,000,000 | 1.64% |
| NGN 2,000,000 | NGN 62,000,000 | 3.23% |
| NGN 5,000,000 | NGN 65,000,000 | 7.69% |

Ownership in a simple primary subscription equals new cash divided by pre-money value plus new cash. Dividing NGN 5 million by NGN 60 million gives 8.33%, but that is not the post-subscription ownership calculation. A purchase of existing shareholders' shares is a different transaction and does not automatically put cash into the Company.
