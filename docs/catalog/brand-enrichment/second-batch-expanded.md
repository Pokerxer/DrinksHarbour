These details explain why the winery talks about the vine as well as the grape variety when describing its wines.

The Sweet Red provides another point of comparison. Its name makes sweetness part of the buying decision, so it can be useful when guests already know the style they like. ### Story

Tomatin dates its first formal distillery to 1897. The producer’s account links the project to the Highland Railway, whose planned route through Tomatin had been announced in 1892. John MacDougall, a local man, began planning the distillery after that announcement. The location gives the business a particular place in the Highlands and helps explain how transport and local enterprise came together in its early history.

The first company did not operate continuously. The producer records a closure in 1906, followed by a reopening in 1909 under new ownership. The reopened distillery started with two stills. Those details give the history a clearer shape than a founding date alone. A registered distillery, a closure and a restart all formed part of the business’s early years.

Demand for Scotch whisky rose after the Second World War, and Tomatin expanded in response. The distillery records reaching 23 stills by 1974. That scale is a sharp contrast with the two stills at the reopening and helps show how much the operation grew over the course of the twentieth century. The size of the historic plant is one part of the story. The character of an individual bottling comes down to the spirit and the decisions made during maturation.

The producer describes the house spirit as mellow and fruity. Its account points to naturally soft water from the Alt-na-Frith burn and to the tall, slender copper stills at the distillery. These details explain how the producer presents the character of its spirit before the different casks enter the picture. In the catalog, the 12 Year with bourbon and sherry cask influences, the 14 Year Port Cask and the Italian Amarone expression provide concrete ways to explore that next step.

When tasting the range, use the cask references to ask questions about the glass. Which pour seems fruitier to you? Which leaves a longer finish? How does a little water affect the aroma? Comparing the bottles in small pours at the same temperature gives those questions a practical answer. The distillery’s long history connects the whiskies, while each bottle’s age statement, cask details and strength give you a way to distinguish the experience you are choosing. For a tasting with friends, pour the expressions in separate labelled glasses and share your impressions before reading the distillery’s tasting notes. That gives everyone a chance to describe the differences in their own words.
