2026-11-10: Ran `founder-cfo` on the e-commerce stream in isolation (files: `founder/numbers-ecommerce.json`, `ecommerce-cfo.md`, `ecommerce-cfo-conservative.md`). Unit = ₦100k of GMV; price = 11% blended commission; variable 8% of GMV (processing 2% + delivery subsidy 3% + refunds 1% + acquisition 2%, per `valuation-assumptions.md`). Zero fixed/startup modelled: marketplace shares the platform with the SaaS stream, whose ₦4m/month is covered by tenant-months in `founder/cfo.md`.

Results: contribution ₦3,000 per ₦100k GMV (27% of commission revenue); ramp assumes tenants 4 → 200 with GMV/tenant ₦2k → ₦200k (my estimate — no sales data; recorded fact is zero online sales). Year 1: ₦11.87m commission revenue, ₦3.24m contribution; month 12 at Bull-state GMV (₦40m/mo) gives ₦4.4m revenue / ₦1.2m contribution, matching the Bull row of `profit-scenarios.csv`. Cross-check passed.

Key points: no break-even constraint — every incremental tenant is pure upside on a linear GMV engine, but 3% of GMV is thin: conservative run at the 9% rate (Enterprise/Venue mix) drops year-1 contribution to ₦1.08m, and Unit costs +15% at the 9% rate turns the margin negative. Watch lines: commission compression (tenant-chosen rate vs the fixed 8% cost stack) and whether tenant growth raises GMV/tenant or just splits the same demand.

Standing caveats unchanged from the 2026-10-31 agreements: selling-price-markup tenants pay the platform via markup share, not commission — GMV mix across revenue models changes per-GMV economics; tenant payout stays retail (see docs/memory/selling-price-markup.md). Discounts consume platform share and cannot undercut payout. Not financial advice.
