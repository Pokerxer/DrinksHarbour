2026-11-10: Follow-up to `marketplace-ecommerce-scaling.md` — replaced the 3%-of-GMV delivery subsidy with per-order economics after public research. Run with `founder-cfo`; sources in `founder/ecommerce-sources.md`.

Facts gathered: typical bottle prices ₦6,600–₦172,000 across DrinksHarbour storefront and Lagos/Abuja competitors; a mixed order ≈ **₦40k AOV (my estimate)**. Abuja bike dispatch is a **fixed ₦1,500–₦9,500 per drop** (~₦3,500 median) — a "% of GMV" delivery subsidy is only coherent at AOV ≳ ₦55k.

Results (order-level, commission 11%, processing ₦700, refunds ₦400, acquisition ₦800 at ₦40k AOV):
- Base (customer pays delivery, per the 2026-10-17 agreement): **contribution ₦2,500/order (6.5% of AOV)**; year-1 contribution ₦6.75m on ₦11.87m commission revenue (57% margin at plan; ~900 orders year 1).
- Stress (platform subsidises delivery at ₦3,500 real drop cost): **−₦1,000/order, margin never breaks even**; year 1 −₦2.7m. **Break-even AOV under full delivery subsidy ≈ ₦55,385** (solve 0.065·AOV ≥ ₦3,600). Premium/case/gifting baskets (stream 6, clear event pre-orders) are where subsidised delivery can work; small to-customer orders must stay customer-pays.
- Stream 3 ads (self-serve dashboard banners, attach 2% of GMV — my conservative estimate): 90% margin, zero fixed; at Base GMV ₦10m → ~₦200k/mo, at Bull ₦40m → ₦800k/mo, year-1 ₦1.94m contribution. Infrastructure already exists (`popup_banner_clickthrough.md`). Value is strictly a function of GMV/demand.

Standing caveats: GMV ramp is an estimate (zero online sales recorded); selling-price-markup tenants change per-GMV economics; ads attach % unproven — replace with real traffic/advertiser data.
